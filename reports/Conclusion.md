## 10. Conclusion
I selected the MLP with one 64-unit hidden layer, MAE loss and SGD at a learning rate of 0.01. Its best checkpoint was epoch 40 after completing 100 epochs. The average absolute error was **5.004 minutes**, compared with **8.823** for the training-median baseline and **5.659** for the distance-only model. The MLP also improved on linear regression. Adding another hidden layer did not improve the result. Across three seeds, the selected configuration averaged 5.013 ± 0.010 minutes.

The biggest problem was a small group of very long recorded trips. Just **15 trips contributed 98.98% of squared error**, pushing RMSE to 54.859 minutes and leaving R-squared at 0.026. I kept these trips in the main evaluation. For example, one record lasted about 1,488 minutes, while my model predicted about 11 minutes. Its pickup time was 23:22 in New York, and this single record contributed about 7.1 minutes to the 23:00 group's 9.5-minute MAE. This explains why the all-row hourly ranking can be misleading. On trips up to 180 minutes, overall MAE was **2.895 minutes**. I report that as an additional diagnostic, and I still need the new subgroup outputs before identifying the hardest hour or distance group for this subset.

Distance provided most of the predictive information. Removing it increased MAE by **1.094 minutes**, while removing weather increased MAE by **0.089 minutes**. Weather helped a little in this matched run, although I used only one seed for each feature comparison. The additional dropout DNN did not improve validation MAE. It reduced the final training/validation gap, but dropout itself affects the training metric, so the smaller gap alone does not show better prediction accuracy.

For extra credit, I rebuilt the DNN in PyTorch. TensorFlow and PyTorch reached MAE values of **5.042 and 5.055 minutes**, respectively. TensorFlow was slightly better in this run, but the small difference was of a similar order to the observed seed variation. On Colab CPU, TensorFlow took **69.3 seconds** and PyTorch took **151.9 seconds**. These times compare my implementations and execution settings. I used Keras `fit` and callbacks for TensorFlow, and an explicit training loop with checkpoint saving for PyTorch.

This experiment supports the MLP for the data and settings I tested. Its main limitations are the suspicious timestamps, five negative predictions and the change in travel patterns between early and later 2020. I used validation for both model selection and reporting, so a separate test set is the next evaluation step. A system predicting before pickup would also need estimated route distance and weather available at prediction time, rather than recorded trip distance and retrospective weather.
