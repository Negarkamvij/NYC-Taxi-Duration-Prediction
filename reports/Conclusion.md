## 10. Conclusion
I selected the MLP with one 64-unit hidden layer, MAE loss and SGD at a learning rate of 0.01. It completed 100 epochs, and the best checkpoint was epoch 40. Its all-row validation MAE was **5.004 minutes**, compared with 8.823 for the training-median baseline, 5.659 for the distance-only model and 5.421 for the best linear model. The best DNN reached 5.042 minutes, so adding a second hidden layer did not improve my result. Across three seeds, the selected configuration averaged 5.013 ± 0.010 minutes. Its mean was 0.029 minutes below the next configuration, although three seeds give limited evidence about stability.

The all-row RMSE was **54.859 minutes**, MSE was 3009.546 minutes squared and R-squared was 0.026. These results show a serious limitation: 15 validation trips above 180 minutes contributed 98.98% of squared error and 42.24% of absolute error. I kept these records and included them in the primary scores. Their recorded durations are close to a full day, which suggests possible timestamp problems that I have not verified. On the additional subset of trips up to 180 minutes, MAE was **2.895 minutes**, RMSE was 5.541 and R-squared was 0.715. This subset does not replace the all-row evaluation. Five predictions were negative, and I kept them unclipped in the metrics.

The long records also distort errors by hour, distance and weather. For example, the highest all-row hourly MAE does not establish that this hour is normally hardest to predict. I added the same subgroup tables for trips up to 180 minutes so that I can examine the pattern separately. I would check sample counts and both sets of tables before drawing conclusions about any group. The rain and dry comparison is observational and does not establish a weather effect.

Distance provided more predictive information than weather in my matched comparisons. Removing distance increased all-row MAE to 6.098 minutes, a change of +1.094. Removing weather increased it to 5.093 minutes, a smaller change of +0.089. These variants each used one seed, so I would repeat them before claiming a reliable improvement from weather. Actual recorded distance is available after the ride, so a system making predictions before pickup would need an estimated route distance instead.

Dropout at 0.1 **did not improve validation MAE** in the additional DNN experiment: MAE changed from 5.042 to 5.046 minutes. The final validation-minus-training MAE gap decreased from 1.024 to 0.472 minutes. Since dropout is active during training and inactive during validation, the smaller gap alone does not prove better generalization. The original DNN also showed overfitting: its final validation MAE was 5.381, compared with 5.042 at its best checkpoint.

For extra credit, the matched TensorFlow DNN reached MAE 5.042 minutes and PyTorch reached 5.055 minutes. TensorFlow was slightly better in this run. The difference of 0.014 minutes is small and is of a similar order to the seed variation, so I cannot claim a clear framework advantage. The measured Colab CPU times were 69.3 seconds for TensorFlow and 151.9 seconds for PyTorch, making this PyTorch implementation about 2.2 times slower. Execution settings and the explicit batch loop may contribute, but I did not isolate the causes. TensorFlow used `fit` and callbacks, while PyTorch used an explicit training loop and saved `state_dict`. Different initialization and execution details mean that matching seeds do not guarantee identical results.

My training sample was 84.9% January–March, while validation covered later 2020 travel. Removing month and year-progress features did not remove this change in the data distribution. I also used validation for model selection and final reporting, so these scores may be optimistic. I did not evaluate a separate test set. My results support the selected MLP within this experiment, while timestamp quality and evaluation on new data remain important limitations.
