## 10. Conclusion
I selected the **MLP** with **MAE loss, SGD, learning rate 0.01, and checkpoint epoch 53**. It trained for all 100 epochs. Its all-row validation MAE was **5.010 minutes**, RMSE was **54.857 minutes**, MSE was **3009.240 minutes squared**, and R-squared was **0.026**. I did not clip predictions at zero; **20 predictions were negative**.

My training-median baseline had MAE **8.823**, and the distance-only Keras baseline had **5.659**. The best original-grid linear, MLP and DNN scores were **5.423, 5.010, and 5.053 minutes**. The selected MLP beat both baselines and the linear model. The deeper network did not improve this result. Across three seeds, the leading configuration averaged **5.014 ± 0.004 minutes**. The gap to the next mean was **0.029**, compared with a larger standard deviation of **0.016**. This supports the MLP choice in these runs, but three seeds are a small comparison.

MAE gave my lowest validation MAE: the best MAE-trained and MSE-trained grid scores were **5.010** and **6.118 minutes**. MAE targets the conditional median, while MSE targets the conditional mean and gives very long trips much more influence. I kept **15 validation trips above 180 minutes**. They contributed **98.98% of squared error**, **42.18% of absolute error**, and **2.114 minutes** of the total MAE. On trips at most 180 minutes, MAE was **2.901**, RMSE was **5.535**, and R-squared was **0.716**. These are additional metrics, not replacements for the primary all-row results. The new largest-errors table confirms several near-24-hour durations. I suspect timestamp/date issues in those records, but I have not verified the individual rides.

My largest distance-bucket MAE was in **>20 miles (11.844 minutes)**. The highest hourly MAE was at **23:00 (9.495 minutes)**. Rain-group MAE was **2.923**, compared with **5.295** for dry weather. The dry group contains the large recorded-duration errors, so this is not evidence that rain makes trips easier to predict.

Without distance, MAE was **6.086 minutes**, a change of **+1.076**. Without weather, it was **5.085**, a change of **+0.074**. Distance added more predictive information than weather in these matched runs. I would need estimated distance before a real trip. The dropout DNN lowered its all-row MAE from **5.053 to 5.025**; its final train/validation gap changed from **0.212 to -0.062 minutes**.

For extra credit, the matched TensorFlow DNN had MAE **5.053**, and PyTorch had **5.010 minutes**. TensorFlow used `fit` and checkpoint callbacks; I wrote the PyTorch batch loop and saved `state_dict` when validation MAE improved. Keras Dense used Glorot uniform initialization. PyTorch Linear used fan-in uniform initialization implemented with `kaiming_uniform_(a=sqrt(5))`, so the same seed did not mean the same starting weights. The saved local-CPU reference runs took **29.3 and 31.0 seconds**, respectively, including checkpointing and reporting. The TensorFlow reference was a retrain, not a copied grid timing. Sources: [Keras Dense](https://keras.io/2/api/layers/core_layers/dense/) and [PyTorch Linear implementation](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/torch/nn/modules/linear.py).

My training rows were **84.9% January–March**, while validation covered later 2020 travel. Removing month and year-progress inputs did not remove the COVID-related distribution shift. I also used validation for both selecting models and reporting their scores, so the results may be optimistic. I did not add a test set. All requested comparisons, the real PyTorch run, and the optional dropout experiment were completed.
